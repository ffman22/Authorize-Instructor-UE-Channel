# Clip rights and platform policy

## Why this exists
The source workflow downloads viral TikTok clips and reposts them. A clip belongs to its creator: reposting without permission risks copyright claims, strikes, and a demonetized or removed channel. YouTube also demotes repetitive, mass-produced content and expects reused material to carry original commentary. Instagram removes unoriginal reposts from recommendations.

## Allowed `rights` values (manifest)
| Value | Meaning | Proof to log |
|---|---|---|
| `own` | Filmed or generated by the owner | none |
| `licensed` | Stock or licensed library | license URL |
| `permission` | Creator agreed in writing (DM, email) | link or screenshot path in `proof` |
| `cc` | Creative Commons that allows reuse | license + attribution in `credit` |

A clip without one of these does not render. `tools/rights_gate.py` enforces it.

## Clip sources that fit
- Creators who run "send us clips" programs or licensing marketplaces for viral video.
- Own footage and AI-generated clips (Higgsfield, Veo-class tools), labeled as such where the platform requires it.
- CC-licensed archives. Keep the credit in the description.

## Original layer
Every video gives original technical instruction, architecture breakdown or gameplay animation analysis by the owner. Never re-upload raw third-party clips.

## Disclosure
- Use the platform's altered/synthetic content label for AI-generated footage.
- Put `credit` lines in the description for `cc` and `permission` clips.

## Volume
Vary topics, formats and hooks; do not publish near-identical videos. At most 1 Short per day under 100,000 subscribers.
